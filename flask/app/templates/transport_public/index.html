How the relay works

A dog picked up in rural Mississippi doesn't need one driver to take them all the way to their foster in Ohio. Instead, the route is split into legs — usually 2–4 hours each — and each leg is driven by a different volunteer. Drivers meet at a prearranged handoff point, the dog moves to the next car, and the relay continues.

Coordinators plan the routes and assign legs using our private transport tracker. Drivers receive the details for their specific leg only, and log checkpoint updates along the way. Real-time coordination happens over the network's group text thread — the tracker is for status logging and record-keeping.
