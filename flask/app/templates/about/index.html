About the network

We are a coordination layer for animal rescue — not a shelter, not an adoption agency. We connect the dogs, people, and organizations already doing the work.

What we do

The National Animal Rescue Network partners with shelters and animal control centers in underserved areas — facilities that are overcrowded, underfunded, or simply overlooked by the mainstream rescue community. We match dogs from those facilities with vetted foster homes, coordinate multi-leg transport routes to move them there, and connect them with adopters once they're ready.

We don't run the shelters. We don't evaluate every dog ourselves. We build the coordination infrastructure that makes the relay possible: the partner network, the transport logistics, the foster and adoption matching, and the public visibility that gets dogs looked at twice.

How we're structured

The network is run by a core team and a group of state-level champions — one per state — who coordinate local fosters, transporters, and partner relationships in their area. Champions are network-appointed advocates, distinct from any elected office. Each state champion is responsible for knowing what's happening on the ground in their state and connecting local resources to the national relay.
