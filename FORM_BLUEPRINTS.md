# Google Forms blueprints

## Global rules for every form

- Use a separate form for each workflow below; do not create one giant application.
- Name the organization and explain the form’s purpose before the first question.
- Identify what happens next and give a realistic response-time placeholder for the owner to finalize.
- Link the privacy notice and identify the data-retention owner.
- Collect only information needed for the decision.
- Never collect Social Security numbers, passwords, payment card/bank details, or unnecessary identity documents.
- Turn on response receipts where appropriate. Avoid publishing response summaries.
- Link responses to a private Sheet. Restrict the Sheet independently from the Form.
- Use file-upload questions only on restricted forms for approved partners/evaluators. File upload requires Google sign-in.
- Add internal workflow columns—ID, owner, stage, decision, last action, notes—to the private tracker, not to the respondent-facing form.
- Confirmation text should say that submission is not approval and should list the official follow-up channel.

## 1. Adoption Application

Audience: public. File upload: no. Branch by housing status and current pets.

Sections and fields:

1. **Dog and applicant** — Dog ID/name; legal name; preferred name; email; phone; city; state; ZIP; preferred contact method; age confirmation.
2. **Household** — adults; children/ages; all household members agree; housing type; own/rent/other; landlord or property restrictions; yard/fence; planned living arrangement.
3. **Current animals** — species, age, sex, sterilization status, temperament; veterinarian name/contact; permission to contact; if none, explanation.
4. **Experience and plan** — prior dogs; training approach; daily schedule; hours alone; exercise; travel; care during emergencies; anticipated life changes; dog expectations.
5. **Eligibility and consent** — willingness to interview, provide references, meet, and complete home verification where applicable; accuracy certification; privacy consent; typed acknowledgment.

Confirmation: “Thank you. This application has been received for review. Submission does not guarantee placement. The participating shelter or rescue will contact you through the information provided if the application moves forward.”

## 2. Foster Application

Audience: public. File upload: no. Branch based on preferred placement types.

Fields: contact and location; age confirmation; household; housing permission; current pets; animal experience; availability; maximum duration; emergency/short-term/long-term/medical/decompression preferences; size/age restrictions; isolation space; transport ability; administering medication; supplies needed; schedule; travel; veterinarian; references; emergency contact; training/support agreement; home-check consent; photo/publicity consent as optional and separate; privacy and accuracy acknowledgment.

Ask the applicant to distinguish “can accept now” from “generally interested.” Ongoing availability updates should use a shorter approved-foster form after onboarding.

## 3. General Volunteer Application

Audience: public. File upload: no. Use checkboxes followed by conditional sections.

Common fields: name; email; phone; city/state/ZIP; age confirmation; preferred contact; availability; languages; accessibility needs; relevant experience; emergency contact; screening/training consent; code-of-conduct acknowledgment.

Role branches:

- **Transport:** vehicle/mode, license and insurance willingness, radius, states/corridors, overnight availability, lifting/handling comfort.
- **Photography:** camera/equipment, portfolio URL, editing experience, service radius, availability, rights/release acknowledgment.
- **Advocacy:** topics, state/district, speaking/writing/social skills, non-harassment and accuracy pledge.
- **Events:** event experience, lifting/setup ability, fundraising/cash-handling interest, schedule.
- **State Champion:** do not collect this inline. Point applicants to the dedicated State Champion Application (form 13 below) instead — this is a distinct advocacy role, not a checkbox on the general form. Never call this role "state representative" — that's the title of an actual elected official.
- **Administrative/general:** skills, software, scheduling, data entry, outreach, grant research.

## 4. Dog Evaluator Application

Audience: public. File upload: no during initial interest stage.

Fields: contact/location; service radius; relevant employment/volunteer background; years and settings of dog-handling experience; credentials with issuing body and expiry; behavior/evaluation methods; experience with fearful, barrier-reactive, resource-guarding, high-arousal, and bite-history cases; safety scenario questions; availability; travel; equipment; professional references; conflict disclosure; willingness to use network protocol; no-guarantee acknowledgment; confidentiality; training/screening consent.

Do not publicly imply that a credential is valid until verified.

## 5. Shelter/Rescue Partner Application

Audience: public or invite link. File upload: avoid on the first form.

Fields: legal/public organization names; organization type; EIN/registration where applicable; physical and mailing locations; website/social links; primary and backup contacts; service area; animals served; intake/adoption model; veterinary relationship; insurance; licensing/inspection information; transport practices; euthanasia/placement disclosures as required by policy; references; requested network services; agreement and verification consent; authorized submitters.

After approval, send restricted upload and update links.

## 6. Submit or Update a Dog

Audience: approved partners only. File upload: yes, if organization-owned Google account policies allow it.

Fields: submitter identity; partner ID; new/update/remove; existing Dog ID; dog name; location; legal custody/authority confirmation; intake/source context; age/sex/size/breed estimate; sterilization/vaccination/microchip status; medical conditions/medications; behavior observations; bite/incident disclosure; compatibility with dogs/cats/children as Yes/No/Unknown; housing deadline; foster/transport need; adoption requirements; public bio; photo/video uploads; photo-rights confirmation; application URL; sponsorship eligibility/goal; urgent safety notes; certification and update commitment.

All submissions enter private review. Removal/adopted updates should be fast and prominent.

## 7. Rescue Availability Update

Audience: approved partners only. File upload: no.

Fields: partner ID; submitter; current status Open/Limited/Waitlist/Closed; effective date; next review date; animal types; size/age/medical/behavior constraints; number or range if the partner wants it public; transport support; geographic limits; public contact link; internal notes; approval to publish selected fields.

Expire availability unless reconfirmed.

## 8. Transport Request

Audience: verified partners only. File upload: yes for required records, or use a restricted Drive folder.

Fields: requesting partner; coordinator contact; dog IDs/count; origin city/state; destination city/state; sending organization/contact; receiving organization/contact; custody/acceptance confirmations; requested date/window; reason; in-state/interstate/aviation/international; animal size/temperament/handling needs; crate/equipment; health certificate/vaccination requirements; veterinary clearance; overnight need; route flexibility; funds requested and estimate; records upload; emergency contact; accuracy and cancellation acknowledgment.

Exact handoff addresses and route timing stay private.

## 9. Dog Evaluation Submission

Audience: approved evaluators only. File upload: yes.

Fields: evaluator identity/ID; assignment ID; Dog ID; date/time/location; people present; environment; handling precautions; objective observations by protocol section; body-language observations; response to handling, leash, novel stimuli, dogs, resources, confinement, and recovery; safety incidents; limitations; recommended conditions; further evaluation needed; overall result; report upload; video/photo upload; confidentiality and accuracy certification.

Avoid definitive temperament guarantees. Provide an immediate safety-contact instruction above the submit button.

## 10. Event/Fundraiser Proposal

Audience: public. File upload: optional only after initial review.

Fields: organizer contact; organization/company; event type; proposed date/time/location; concept; audience and expected attendance; requested dogs/volunteers/brand use; fundraising method; proposed beneficiary/designation; expenses; venue permission; insurance/permit status; animal-safety plan; cash/payment handling; publicity plan; accessibility; cancellation plan; agreement that the event is not authorized until written approval.

## 11. Newsletter Signup

Audience: public. Keep it short.

Fields: email; first name optional; state optional; explicit consent checkbox; optional interest choices. State expected monthly frequency and link privacy information. Do not pre-check consent.

Use an email platform with unsubscribe and suppression management. A Form response Sheet is a source record, not the complete mailing system.

## 12. Contact and News Submission

Audience: public. Branch by topic.

Fields: name; email; state; topic—general, media, local news/story, resource suggestion, website correction, urgent listing update, other; organization; subject; message; source URL; permission to contact; permission to publish story content (separate, optional); attachment only if policies support it.

Display a notice if the organization is not a 24/7 emergency service. Provide appropriate crisis resources instead of implying immediate response.

## 13. State Champion Application

Audience: public. File upload: no.

A "State Champion" is the network's own advocacy role, not an elected office — never call it "state representative" or "state senator" on this form, since those are the actual titles of real elected officials the site also references elsewhere (see the state-champions mini-app's Congressional Delegation section). One Champion per state builds relationships with their state's actual legislators and shows up for animal-welfare and shelter/rescue policy on the network's behalf. It is deliberately a separate, fuller application from the General Volunteer Application, the same way the Dog Evaluator Application is separate from it.

Sections and fields:

1. **Contact and state** — legal name; email; phone; city; which state they'd represent; age confirmation; preferred contact method.
2. **Why this role** — why they want to advocate for the network and the dogs in their state; any existing relationships with state legislators, agencies, or animal-welfare coalitions; relevant legislative, policy, or community-organizing experience.
3. **Capacity** — weekly/monthly time available; willingness to travel to the state capitol; willingness to speak publicly or testify if asked; comfort representing the organization's positions rather than personal opinions.
4. **Commitment and conduct** — acknowledgment that this role advocates on animal-welfare policy, not on behalf of any candidate, party, or ballot measure; conflict-of-interest disclosure (e.g. current employment or lobbying work on related issues); separate, optional consent to a public photo and short bio on the site; acknowledgment that an organization email address is required once appointed; term acknowledgment (e.g. renewable annually; the network can end the arrangement at any time).
5. **Eligibility and consent** — accuracy certification; privacy consent; typed acknowledgment.

Confirmation: "Thank you for applying to represent The National Animal Rescue Network and the dogs in your state. This application has been received for review. Submission does not guarantee appointment. If your state's seat is open and your background is a fit, a coordinator or board member will follow up through the information provided."

Do not publish an applicant's name, photo, or bio anywhere until they are actually appointed and have separately consented to publication — the "public photo and bio" checkbox on this form covers appointment-time publication, not this application itself.
